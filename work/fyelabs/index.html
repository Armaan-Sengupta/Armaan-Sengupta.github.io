FYELABS

Robotics & Embedded Intern · Ontario · May – Sep 2023

An autonomous back-up safety system for industrial garbage trucks: 10 LiDARs, 3 depth cameras, an IR camera, IMU and GPS across 6 microcontrollers.

  • Built back-up safety sensing for garbage trucks
  • Designed a LiDAR dust filter that still detects people
  • Designed the 100+ part enclosure and cut its cost by 56%

Designed a LiDAR dust-filtering algorithm from field data, five parallel filters that reject dust and still catch people (C++). Designed the HMI: a 2-layer PCB with two MCUs, a LoRa link with custom checksums, a graphics library and 12-zone object mapping. Designed the 100+ part actuating stainless enclosure over 15 revisions, cutting cost 56% and halving lead time.

The problem

Industrial garbage trucks reversing in harsh, dirty conditions need to reliably avoid people and objects. The sensing unit on the back of the truck carries 10 LiDARs, 3 depth cameras, an infrared camera, a light sensor, an IMU and GPS, run by 3 microcontrollers and 4 mini compute units, all in a weatherproof housing that protects the sensors when the truck isn't reversing.

The enclosure

I designed the all-metal, actuating enclosure: over 100 parts across 15 major revisions covering electronics, sensor placement and manufacturability. Redesigning most of the structure as 6 sheet-metal parts, cut, bent and welded, instead of CNC milling cut manufacturing cost by 56% and halved lead time. My gasket and geometry design kept the electronics dry in a water-spray test.

The HMI

The dashboard HMI is the driver's link to the unit on the back of the truck. I did all of it: the plastic case, a custom 2-layer PCB with two microcontrollers (one for the wireless link; one for 4 buttons, a speaker and a 7" display), and the firmware for both. After testing several wireless options I built a custom LoRa protocol with checksums for reliable data across 6 microcontrollers, plus a 12-zone mapping that shows the driver where a detected object is.

LiDAR dust filtering

The LiDAR array is 10 single-beam sensors in an arc, which is robust with no moving parts but too sparse for standard point-cloud filtering, and dust was causing false positives. I collected distance and return-intensity data in clean and dusty conditions; from it, my supervisors and I developed five filters that run in parallel to ignore dust and still catch a person. I implemented them in C++ firmware on the LiDAR board.

A visualiser for tuning

To tune the filters I wrote a Python LiDAR visualiser: first a serial interceptor, then a wireless MQTT version so the unit could be fully sealed during tests, with recording to a custom file format and real-time playback. It let the whole team help tune the filter constants.
